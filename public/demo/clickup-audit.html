47 records. The other 333 feed entries are either stored and hidden (automation), or never recorded at all (attachments, checklists, dependencies, time tracking, watchers, description saves).

Why this task and not an average one. Across the space automation is 9.2% of stored events. On this task it is 67% — and that is exactly why this task generated a complaint and an average one did not. The tasks people brought to the audit were the automation-heavy ones; sizing the problem off the space average would have missed what made it expensive.

What this demo is

        
A working recreation of the internal ClickUp audit trail described in the case study. One stored record set is the source of truth; every screen here is a view of it. The webhook, the ClickUp API and the database are simulated; the pipeline order, the field set, the ignore rules and the dedup key are the real ones.

      

      

        
The four decisions it makes visible

        
1. The raw payload is stored before anything is interpreted, so a parsing rule can be replayed over history. Two rules have been.

        
2. One space, checked at ingestion. Filtering at query time would mean storing the whole workspace and hoping the filter holds.

      

      

        
more

        
3. Deduplication is on history_items[].id, the source’s own identifier. A retried delivery must not produce a second timeline entry — a record containing the same change twice is not a worse audit trail, it is an unusable one.

        
4. Automation edits are hidden by default and counted in the open. The count is on the screen whether the switch is on or off.

      

    

    
Every person, task, value, date and figure on this page is invented for the demo. No task, customer or individual’s real activity appears here. The counts reproduce the ones quoted in the case study so the claims can be checked rather than taken on trust — 3,262 stored records of which 9.2% are automation edits, 380 feed entries on the task that took three hours to answer, 41 audit requests of which 2 remain outside the field set, and 0 duplicate records after deduplication.
