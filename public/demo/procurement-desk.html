What this demo is

A working recreation of the M365-native operations pilot described in the case study: three request streams — procurement, shipments and the Thursday presentations — run on one operating contract. Each stream has one canonical list; every screen here is a view of one of them. The lists, flows, cards and mailbox are simulated; the contract, the field rules and the gates are the real ones.

The four rules it makes visible

1. Every request gets a named person within a business day — an owner, an approver or a speaker who has answered. A queue, a team mailbox or silence counts as nobody.

2. Every decision is an event carrying actor, timestamp, source and evidence. A transition without a document reference, an approval without a card response, a confirmation without its token: none can be written.

more

3. The flows refuse rather than warn. A request at or above the finance ceiling cannot be submitted; a second approval card for the same shipment cannot fire; a confirmation link cannot act on any row but the one it was minted for.

4. A check runs every morning against each list, not against what the flows report about themselves. Every failure it has found is on the exceptions screen with what changed because of it.

All organisations, requesters, owners, approvers, presenters, suppliers, items, prices, dates and figures on this page are invented for the demo. No supplier, price, requester or speaker from the real pilot appears here. The 48 completed requests reproduce the counts quoted in section 08 of the case study — 23 procurement requests with 21 assigned inside a business day, 14 shipment requests each decided inside a business day, 11 presentations with 10 confirmed before the day before, 0 ordered or shipped unapproved, 1 silent flow failure caught by the daily check — so the gate screen can be checked rather than taken on trust.
